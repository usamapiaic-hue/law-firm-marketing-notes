Website Notes for Employment Law Firms

Employment firms often serve two very different audiences: workers who think they were treated unfairly, and businesses that need advice. A website that tries to speak to both at once usually speaks to neither.

Separate the two paths

If you represent both sides, give each its own section with its own pages. If you represent only employees, say so in the first line; it builds trust straight away.

Pages employees search for

  • Wrongful termination
  • Unpaid wages and overtime
  • Discrimination and harassment
  • Retaliation for reporting problems

Each page should explain, in plain words, what counts, what evidence helps and what deadlines may apply in your state.

Make the first contact easy

Many callers are still employed and worried about being found out. Offer a short, private form and say clearly how the firm will contact them.
